Who built the river diversion?

Many people say the São Francisco river diversion is Bolsonaro's work. Others say it is Lula's alone. Here is what each government did, in simple words.

📊 See the summary in icons

The short answer

The project belongs to several governments. It began in 2007, under Lula, and most of the canals were built under Lula and Dilma. Temer inaugurated the East Axis. Bolsonaro finished the North Axis and one of the branch canals. With Lula back, the remaining branches continue. When Bolsonaro took office, more than 90% of the two main canals were already finished.

How it works

Pumps draw water from the river and send it along canals to reservoirs in the semi-arid backlands. From there, branch canals and pipelines carry the water to the cities.

What each government did

The river diversion was like a relay race: each government picked up the project at one point and passed it on.

Before

The idea is old

  • Emperor Pedro II ordered a study of the project. The report came out in 1860.
  • Today's project began to be designed in 1985.
2007

Lula 2: construction begins

  • Construction began in 2007, with a promise of completion in 2012.
2011

Dilma: most of the canals

  • She renegotiated the contracts in 2011 and 2012, after the construction companies asked for more money.
  • About 80% of the two canals were built under Lula and Dilma.
2017

Temer: the East Axis starts working

  • In March 2017, the East Axis began operating.
2019

Bolsonaro: finishes the North Axis

  • He received the canals more than 90% finished.
  • He inaugurated the stretch of the North Axis that carries water to Ceará, in 2020. Both canals were finished in 2021.
  • He delivered the Agreste Branch, in Pernambuco, in October 2021.
2023

Lula 3: the missing branches

  • In 2024 he inaugurated the pumping station of the Agreste Pipeline. Without it, the Agreste Branch did not carry water to the cities.
  • He moved ahead with the Apodi Branch, which carries water to Rio Grande do Norte, and the Salgado Branch, in Ceará.
  • Lula promised to finish the project in 2026.

What is still missing

The two main canals already work. Branches that carry the water farther are still missing. Status in July 2026:

The project was promised for 2012. It has already gone through six governments and is still not finished.

Is it true?

False

“The river diversion is Bolsonaro's work.” The project began in 2007. When he took office, more than 90% of the two canals were already finished. He completed important stretches, but did not build it alone.

False

“Bolsonaro did nothing on the river diversion.” He finished the North Axis, delivered the Agreste Branch and spent R$3.5 billion from 2019 to 2021.

Half true

“The river diversion was Lula's idea.” The idea dates back to Emperor Pedro II, and the current project is from 1985. Lula is the one who got construction started.

False

“Lula re-inaugurated a work Bolsonaro had already delivered.” In 2024 Lula inaugurated the Agreste Pipeline pumping station, which is a different structure. Bolsonaro had delivered the Agreste Branch in 2021.

False

“Lula ordered the river diversion pumps shut off.” Comprova checked and it is not true. One of the stations broke down in November 2022 and stopped for repairs in 2023.

False

“The project is already finished.” The main canals work, but the Apodi and Salgado branches were still under construction in July 2026.

True

“The project was very late.” It was supposed to be finished in 2012.

In short

  • The river diversion does not have a single owner. It went through Lula, Dilma, Temer, Bolsonaro and Lula again.
  • Lula and Dilma carried out an important part of the canals; the financial table separates annual periods, with 2016 shared between Dilma and Temer.
  • Bolsonaro finished the North Axis and the Agreste Branch.
  • In the July 2026 snapshot, stretches of the branches were still missing.

How much was ready and when

Poder360 analyzed reports from 2018 and 2019 and concluded that about 80% of the two canals were built under Lula and Dilma. Comprova calculated that at least 92.5% of the physical work on the two canals was done when Bolsonaro took office. The stretch he inaugurated in Ceará in 2020 was already more than 80% done in 2016. The ministry says finished parts deteriorated and needed repairs, and Poder360 found 9 extra works since 2016, costing R$50.5 million.

What each side says

In 2022, Bolsonaro said water-tanker trucks in the Northeast fell 30% “after the river diversion that we built was completed”. Lula calls it “the project of the century” and said in 2025 that it “should already have been finished”, blaming those who supported Dilma's removal. Former minister Rogério Marinho said the project “has no owner, it belongs to all Brazilians” and blamed mistakes by previous administrations for the delay.

2025 works

In May 2025, the government planned to deliver the first stretch of the Apodi Branch, in Paraíba, and sign the order to double the EBI-3 pumping station, in Salgueiro (Pernambuco), from 24.75 to 49 thousand liters per second. Ceará's Cinturão das Águas canal was 83.5% done.

Movimento Econômico

Cisterns

Another way to bring water to the backlands. From 2003 to 2018 (Lula, Dilma and Temer), 929,000 drinking-water cisterns were delivered. From January 2019 to June 2022 (Bolsonaro), 37,600.
